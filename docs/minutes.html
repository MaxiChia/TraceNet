Minutes of Meeting

Meeting 1 — 7 June 2026

Date: 7 June 2026
Time: 10:00 – 10:45pm
Location: Teams Call
Participants: Brandon Komajaya, Albert Chia, Chua Wee Tian

Agenda

  1. Discussion on project topic
  2. Action items

Discussion

Review of Mini-Challenge 1 (MC1) and Mini-Challenge 2 (MC2)

The main goal of the meeting was to decide whether to proceed with MC1 or MC2 for the group project. Each team member presented their respective Take-Home Exercise 02 findings.

MC1 Presentation — Brandon: The challenge involved an information leak at a company on 5 June. A private chat channel (“shadow channel”) was created on 22 March, which the AI agent (“Judge”) could not access. An intern not part of the private chat requested and was granted permission to release information, leading to the leak. The AI agent could not alert supervisors due to its lack of visibility into the private channel.

MC2 Presentation — Albert: An in-depth analysis tracing the anomalous SaidIT post back through a delegation chain of nine agents to the earliest visible initiator. The analysis was noted as detailed, with confirmed detection rule (content_source IS NOT NULL) and a proposed single intervention point.

MC2 Presentation — Wee Tian: Identified the three anomalous files (HiddenOrca.txt, MellowOtter.txt, SwiftWren.txt) and recommended a control point for file verification before posting.

Group project direction

Brandon suggested MC2 might offer more visually impressive outputs for a Shiny app. Albert noted MC1 had strong, clear visualisations — micro timeline, network graph — that would translate well to an interactive format. A concern was raised that MC2’s complexity might be difficult to present effectively. The team referenced the Big Fish example poster, noting its emphasis on network diagrams as a guide for the final project’s visual direction.

Conclusion

The team could not reach a final decision on which Mini-Challenge to pursue. It was agreed to review each other’s work again before holding a poll on 8 June 2026.

Action Items

1 Review each other’s Mini-Challenge submissions All 8 Jun 2026
2 Conduct a poll over Telegram to decide on MC1 or MC2 All 8 Jun 2026
NoteAfternote

Following a poll conducted over Telegram on 8 June 2026, the team reached a consensus to proceed with Mini-Challenge 2 for the group project, based on the combined familiarity of two team members with the MC2 dataset and the strength of the existing analytical work.

Meeting 2 — 20 June 2026

Date: 20 June 2026
Time: 10:10 – 10:45pm
Location: Teams Call
Participants: Brandon Komajaya, Albert Chia, Chua Wee Tian

Agenda

  1. Final project deliverables and submission requirements
  2. Shiny app development plan and work division
  3. Methodology and findings preparation
  4. Poster and user manual development
  5. Coordination schedule and checkpoints

Discussion

Final project deliverables

The team reviewed the full submission package due in two weeks. The submission consists of a Shiny app, a poster, a user guide, and meeting minutes. The core deliverable is the Shiny app — the poster will be created using screenshots and graphics from the completed application, and the 7–8 page user guide will serve as a post-development walkthrough document. The team acknowledged a heavy schedule with upcoming quizzes and presentations posed a risk to the timeline, and agreed to prioritise app development accordingly.

Shiny app development and work division

The primary goal is to convert the team’s existing R code and MC2 analysis into an interactive Shiny app with more graphics and fewer words. The app is planned with a modular structure:

  • Module 1 — System topology: agent network, relay chain, incident timeline
  • Module 2 — Normal vs anomalous post differentiation
  • Module 3 — Actor analysis and participation heatmaps

The main technical challenge identified is converting the existing R code to be Shiny-compatible. Albert will begin building Shiny App and share any immediate blockers. If the conversion proves difficult, the work will be broken up and redistributed among the team.

Methodology and findings preparation

To support app development and downstream deliverables, the team will separate methodological documentation and preliminary findings into four distinct files. Brandon is tasked with translating Albert’s MC2 analysis into structured methodology and findings sections, including draft text insights, to be delivered by 22 June 2026. This will provide Albert with reference content for the Shiny app and unblock downstream tasks.

Poster and user manual development

Poster creation is deprioritised until the Shiny app modules are functional. Once the app is ready, screenshots and content will be used to create the poster, due for upload by 1 July. The user manual will be drafted by Brandon after the app is completed and stabilised. The manual will align with the app’s structure, providing guidance on navigation, inputs, and interpretation of outputs.

Conclusion

The team agreed on a clear division of labour and a phased delivery schedule. Albert leads Shiny app development; Brandon leads methodology documentation and user manual, and Wee Tian can assist in the Poster development. Interim checkpoints are set to monitor progress and adjust workloads.
